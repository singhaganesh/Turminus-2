## Difficulty Explanation

The failure looks like a corrupted job or a broken catalog because a single registered unit expands into dozens of one-character unknown names, while the same unit written as a list works. Agents that only exercise the list form never see the break. The dual-shape contract is documented as intentional, so rejecting the bare-string form removes a required convenience rather than repairing selection. Correct behavior also needs dependency closure and a mode field that tracks the YAML writing shape, not the count of selected units. Frontier models often stop after making the list path green or after normalizing names only at the CLI edge.

## Solution Explanation

Reproduce the bare-string job and compare its selected and unknown lists with the one-element sequence job. Trace how focus values enter the planner, how names are expanded into the build set, and how the plan sheet records the writing mode. Promote a bare string to a single focus name before iteration, walk the catalog for dependency closure on every accepted focus writing, and set the mode from the original YAML shape. Rebuild the driver and regenerate the plan through the normal plan pipeline rather than editing the output file.

## Verification Explanation

Checks rebuild the driver and invoke the planner on fresh job documents for bare-string, one-element list, many-element list, and comma-containing bare-string cases. They compare selected sets and closures across matching shapes, require the mode field to follow the writing form, and confirm unknown-name exits and stderr lines when names are missing. Rejecting the bare-string form fails. Hand-written plan files without a working regenerated pipeline also fail.
