Yard census on this image follows `/app/slipfold/SPAN.txt`. The mill writes `/app/inkwell/sheet.toml` with integer field `crossings`: how many distinct bodies have their centroid pass the horizontal scan at `y = 70` from the top of the frame toward the bottom over the full recording. Every clip is 160 by 120 pixels at 12 frames per second. A worked sample is `/app/shown.mp4`; on that file `crossings` is `3`. Kiln notes sit in `/app/kiln/KILN.txt`. Correct the video pipeline modules under `/app`; source implementation work under `/app` is required.

Count a body at most once even when it lingers on the scan or leaves and later returns. Only top-to-bottom centroid passage adds to the total. Bodies that never complete that motion do not add. Similar bodies that share the scan at the same time still count as separate when each centroid completes that passage, including when their silhouettes overlap, one shades the other, or they merge into one region and later split. A long smear of shade that sweeps the scan is not a body and does not add. Static or manual writes of the sheet are not sufficient; the mill must regenerate `/app/inkwell/sheet.toml`. The verifier reruns the mill named in `/app/slipfold/SPAN.txt` on additional recordings that share this size and frame rate. If CLIP cannot be opened, success is false: the process exits with a non-zero status and `/app/inkwell/sheet.toml` is absent.

When the true distinct-crossing count is 5 or fewer, the reported integer must match exactly. When the true count is 6 or more, the reported integer may differ by at most 1 inclusive. Use Python with cv2, numpy, and toml only. Stay offline.
